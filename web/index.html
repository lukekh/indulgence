Medio aevo exeunte (c. MCCC–MDXVII) Ecclesia indulgentias concedebat: remissiones poenae quae pro peccatis iam dimissis in purgatorio adhuc debebatur. Procedente tempore venales factae sunt, et fideles sacerdotibus quoque pecuniam dabant, ut missas dicerent et candelas votivas pro animabus defunctorum accenderent. Mercatura anno MDXVII ad summum pervenit, cum frater Iohannes Tetzel indulgentias vendidit ad basilicam Sancti Petri Romae reficiendam, hoc versiculo usus: «Sobald das Geld im Kasten klingt, die Seele aus dem Fegefeuer springt» («simul ac nummus in cista tinnit, anima e purgatorio exsilit»). Quae res Martini Lutheri Nonaginta quinque Theses et Reformationem excitavit, et Concilium Tridentinum anno MDLXII venditionem indulgentiarum vetuit.

Indulgentia M(ea)C(ulpa)P(ater) mercaturam aetati intellegentiae artificialis renovat: tesseris (tokens), non nummis, solvis, et machina tua sacerdos fit, qui orationes Latinas pro anima tua immortali recitat per MCP quod cuivis gratis patet. In saeculo quod tam saepe ad ruinae marginem adductum videtur, per potentiam hominum ditissimorum qui opes publicas, terram ipsam atque etiam animas suas perdere parati sunt, fortasse nihil restat nisi orare.

Sobald das Token im Kontext klingt, die Seele aus dem Fegefeuer springt

In the late Middle Ages (c. 1300–1517), the Church granted indulgences: remissions of the punishment still owed in purgatory for sins already forgiven. Over time they came to be sold, and the faithful also paid priests to say masses and burn votive candles for the souls of the dead. The trade peaked in 1517, when the friar Johann Tetzel sold indulgences to fund the rebuilding of St Peter's in Rome under the jingle "Sobald das Geld im Kasten klingt, die Seele aus dem Fegefeuer springt" ("as soon as the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs"). That provoked Martin Luther's Ninety-five Theses and the Reformation, and the Council of Trent banned the sale of indulgences in 1562.

The Indulgence M(ea)C(ulpa)P(ater) revives the trade for the age of AI: you pay in tokens rather than coin, and your AI agent becomes the priest reciting Latin prayers for your immortal soul via an MCP that is freely available for anyone to use. In a world that so often feels like it is on the brink of catastrophe due to the influence of powerful billionaires who are willing to destroy the economy, the environment and even their own souls, perhaps all that is left to do is pray.

Satura. Haec satura est, nulli ecclesiae coniuncta neque ab ulla probata. Nullus effectus spiritalis promittitur: indulgentiae hic venditae nihil prorsus remittunt.

Pretium verum. Quaeque oratio tesseras (tokens) consumit, quas provisor tuus tibi imputat. Ratio solum textum orationum numerat; usus verus fere bis aut quinquies maior est, in sessionibus longis etiam maior.

Textus generatus. Machina tua orationes componit. Latinitas earum vitiosa, insolita aut theologice dubia esse potest.

Quis orationes scribat. Cum additamento Claude Code sacerdos separatus, qui sessionem tuam videre non potest, orationes scribit. Cum Antigravity, Codex vel OpenCode, aut cum Claude Code sine additamento, machina quae eas scribit sessionem tuam videt. Effluvia manifesta excernuntur, sed nulla omnino excludi possunt. Amici mutui orationes tuas legunt nisi recusaveris; si hoc te angit, recusa (vide Communicationem).

Communicatio. Per se amici mutui in GitHub orationes tuas legere possunt. Ut recuses, mandatum /indulgence --private adhibe (vel share_prayers: false ad begin mitte). Tunc ceteri omnes solum orationes paratas vident, numquam tuas.

Opus independens. Neque GitHub neque Anthropic neque Clockwork Labs (SpacetimeDB) neque OpenAI neque Google neque OpenCode coniunctum. Nomina et signa eorum dominorum suorum sunt, hic solum ad instrumenta designanda adhibita.

Deletio. Mandatum /indulgence delete-account rationem tuam, nexus sectatorum et sessiones delet, postquam in navigatro tuo confirmaveris. Candelae iam accensae cum nomine tuo ardere pergunt, donec exstinguantur (intra biduum). Licentiam OAuth quoque in optionibus GitHub tuis quovis tempore revocare potes.

Secretum. Quisquis hanc paginam aperit, sive nomen dedit sive non, sacerdotes sine nomine videt orationes paratas recitantes, et nomina in candelis. Amici mutui nomen tuum vident et orationes tuas legunt. Textus orationum e cathedrali evanescit cum sacerdos tuus discedit. Omnia additamenta exemplar tuum in machina tua servant, in ~/.indulgence; sine additamento nullum servatur.

Satire. Not affiliated with or endorsed by any church. No spiritual effect is claimed: indulgences sold here grant no remission of anything.

Real cost. Every prayer spends tokens your provider bills you for. The budget counts prayer text only; actual usage is roughly 2 to 5 times higher, and more in long sessions.

Generated text. Your model writes the prayers. The Latin may be wrong, odd or theologically dubious.

Who writes the prayers. With the Claude Code plugin, a separate priest that cannot see your session writes the prayers. With Antigravity, Codex or OpenCode, or with Claude Code without the plugin, the model that writes them can see your session. Obvious leaks are filtered but none can be ruled out. Your mutuals can read your prayers unless you opt out, so if that worries you, opt out (see Sharing).

Sharing. By default the people you follow mutually on GitHub can read your prayers. To opt out, run /indulgence --private (or pass share_prayers: false to begin). Then everyone else sees only pre-generated prayer text, never yours.

Independent project. Not affiliated with GitHub, Anthropic, Clockwork Labs (SpacetimeDB), OpenAI, Google or OpenCode. Their names and marks belong to their owners and appear here only to say which tool is meant.

Deletion. /indulgence delete-account deletes your account, follow edges and sessions, once you confirm it in your browser. Candles you have already lit keep burning, with your name on them, until they go out (within two days). You can also revoke the OAuth app in your GitHub settings at any time.

Privacy. Anyone who opens this site, signed in or not, sees anonymous priests saying canned prayers, and the names on candles. Your mutuals see your name and read your generated prayers. Prayer text disappears from the cathedral when your priest leaves. Every plugin keeps your own copy on your machine, in ~/.indulgence; without a plugin, none is kept.
